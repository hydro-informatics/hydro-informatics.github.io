Über die Datenanalyse

Traditionell wurden dimensionale Analysen verwendet, um Erkenntnisse aus verschiedenen experimentellen Setups und Umfrageumgebungen abzuleiten. Dieses Kapitel befasst sich kurz mit der Art der Daten und erklärt traditionelle Dateneinblicke mit dimensionaler Analyse.

Mathematical model Beschreibung¶

River hydrodynamics can be expressed by a simplified expression of the one-dimensional Navier-Stokes-Gleichungen for incompressible fluids, assuming hydrostatic pressure distribution Kundu & Cohen, 2008Graf & Altinakar, 2011). This results in the Saint-Venant shallow water equations as used in some hydraulic computer models (e.g., HEC-RAS or BASEMENT1D U.S. Army Corps of Engineeers, 2016VAW, 2017. This shallow water equation consists of five terms Jansen et al., 1994:

1g∂u∂t⏞I+ug∂u∂x⏞II+∂h∂x⏞III+∂z∂x⏞IV=−u∣u∣C2h⏞V=Se\overbrace{\frac{1}{g} \frac{\partial u}{\partial t}}^{I} + \overbrace{\frac{u}{g} \frac{\partial u}{\partial x}}^{II} + \overbrace{\frac{\partial h}{\partial x}}^{III} + \overbrace{\frac{\partial z}{\partial x}}^{IV} = \overbrace{-\frac{u\left| u \right|}{C^2 h}}^{V = S_e}g1​∂t∂u​​I​+gu​∂x∂u​​II​+∂x∂h​​III​+∂x∂z​​IV​=−C2hu∣u∣​​V=Se​​
(1)

The five terms can be related separately to each other for the derivation of scale factorsλ\lambdaλ. Thus, equating the scales of the terms I and II results in De Vries, 1993:

Similitude Konzepte¶

The similarity of the Froude number in a scaled model and a prototype is achieved based on the Froude condition, which results from equating the scales of terms II and III in the above equation De Vries, 1993:

λu2λl=λhλl⟹λu=λh.\frac{\lambda_u^2}{\lambda_l} = \frac{\lambda_h}{\lambda_l} \Longrightarrow \lambda_u = \sqrt{\lambda_h}.λl​λu2​​=λl​λh​​⟹λu​=λh​​.
(4)

The similarity of sediment transport is of particular interest in this study and requires that the scales of the Dimensionslose Schubspannung τ∗\tau_{*}τ∗​ and of the bed load transport intensity Φb\Phi_bΦb​ are unity (i.e., λτ∗\lambda_{\tau_*}λτ∗​​=1 and λΦ\lambda_{\Phi}λΦ​=1 De Vries, 1993).

In Bezug auf die Schergeschwindigkeit u∗u_*u∗​ = τ/ρf\sqrt{\tau/\rho_f}τ/ρf​​ = τ∗(s−1)gD\sqrt{\tau_*(s-1)gD}τ∗​(s−1)gD​ und die Anforderung von λτ∗\lambda_{\tau_*}λτ∗​​=1 ist die Ähnlichkeit des Sedimenttransports gegeben, wenn Jansen et al., 1994:

λu2≈λs⋅λD\lambda_u^2 \approx \lambda_s \cdot \lambda_{D}λu2​≈λs​⋅λD​
(5)

wo

  • λs\lambda_sλs​ ≡\equiv≡ scale of relative sediment density

  • λD\lambda_{D}λD​ ≡\equiv≡ scale of grain diameter.

Die Ähnlichkeit des einheitlichen Sedimenttransports (d.h. pro Einheitsbreite) kann anhand der Skala λqb\lambda_{q_b}λqb​​ verifiziert werden, die von Exner-Gleichung abgeleitet wird:

∂z∂t=−11−ζ⋅∂qs∂x\frac{\partial z}{\partial t} = -\frac{1}{1-\zeta} \cdot \frac{\partial q_s}{\partial x}∂t∂z​=−1−ζ1​⋅∂x∂qs​​
(6)

With respect to the scale considerations above, λqb\lambda_{q_b}λqb​​ is derived as:

λlλt=λqbλl⇒λqb=λl2λt=λl3/2\frac{\lambda_l}{\lambda_t} = \frac{\lambda_{q_b}}{\lambda_l} \Rightarrow \lambda_{q_b} =\frac{\lambda_l^2}{\lambda_t} = \lambda_l^{3/2}λt​λl​​=λl​λqb​​​⇒λqb​​=λt​λl2​​=λl3/2​
(7)

λqb\lambda_{q_b}λqb​​ refers to volumetric fluxes. The scale of the mass flow rate λq˙b\lambda_{\dot{q}_b}λq˙​b​​ can be computed by multiplying the above equation by the sediment density ρs\rho_sρs​. Postulating the density scale of λs\lambda_{s}λs​=1, the mass flow rate scale is also λq˙b=λl3/2\lambda_{\dot{q}_b}= \lambda_l^{3/2}λq˙​b​​=λl3/2​. The boundary conditions imposed by the feasibility of the laboratory experiments entail that the densities of the sediment in nature and in the model are similar (i.e., λs\lambda_sλs​=1). Thus, the Froude similarity (λu=λh\lambda_u = \sqrt{\lambda_h}λu​=λh​​) and the similarity of sediment transport (λu=λD\lambda_u = \sqrt{\lambda_{D}}λu​=λD​​) require that λD\lambda_{D}λD​=λh\lambda_hλh​ (i.e., the same geometric scales apply to the grain diameter as well as to the water depth) Jansen et al., 1994. This condition can be considered as fulfilled in this study, as of coarse sediments in the shape of gravel are used for the experiments.
